Approvals and quality gates ​

Two engines stand between a ready release and your testers: approval workflows decide that the right people have signed off, and quality gates decide that the right facts have been recorded. They are configurable data, not code — an organization that configures neither keeps an ungated pipeline; an organization that configures both gets a chain of evidence no one has to remember to collect.

Approval workflows ​

An approval workflow is an ordered chain of groups of steps. Each step demands a signature from one of the five approval roles — QA_ENGINEER, SECURITY_REVIEWER, COMPLIANCE_REVIEWER, APP_OWNER (the product step), or RELEASE_MANAGER (the classic final sign-off). Groups run sequentially; within a group:

  • ALL (the parallel mode) — every step in the group must approve. All pending siblings are decidable at once.
  • ANY (the or-mode) — the first approval satisfies the group; the remaining steps are recorded as skipped, with which signature satisfied them.
  • Conditional steps — a step can carry a trigger, evaluated when the round starts: ENVIRONMENT_EQUALS (for example, only demand compliance when the release targets production) or FEATURE_FLAG_CONTAINS. An unmet condition skips the step — never silently; the rationale is recorded.

A rejection anywhere rejects the round — a no is never outvoted.

The details that matter in practice:

  • Signatures are facts. Approver and role are recorded at decision time; roles change later, the record does not.
  • The release's creator signs nothing — self-approval is refused.
  • Rejection is terminal for the round. The documented rework path is a new round of the same frozen definition — never an un-reject.
  • Rounds are per definition, and the chain is what the manifest freezes: the signed steps, in chain order.
  • A named policy that fails to resolve fails closed: the crossing refuses rather than silently running a weaker chain.

Quality gates ​

Gates are evaluated facts, not workflows: external systems (usually your CI) submit results with evidence, the platform records them, and the release's crossing consults them. The built-in catalog:

GateKindSemantics
unit-tests, integration-tests, sast, dependency-scan, mobile-security-scanPass/failExternally reported binary outcome, with a mandatory evidence URL
coverage-thresholdMinimumA percentage the definition pins (for example, 80)
crash-free-thresholdMinimumMetric-backed: crash-free session rate
open-critical-bugsMaximumMetric-backed: a ceiling, typically 0, fed by feedback triage

How they bind: gate definitions are organization-grain, with an optional ring attachment — a ring-attached definition overrides the organization-wide one for that ring (per-ring gates, PRD's rule).

How they decide:

  • Blocking. While any required gate of the release's ring is pending, failed, or without data, the VALIDATING to READY crossing refuses — and a ring promotion consults the same evaluation for the target ring.
  • NO_DATA is not zero and not a pass. A metric-backed gate with no data yet blocks, distinctly from a failure — no data yet is not a bad result, but it holds all the same.
  • Results are latest-wins with full history. Every submission is appended; the current state mirrors the newest result.
  • Evidence is mandatory. Every externally reported result carries an http(s) URL a human can follow — the report link, not a claim.
  • Overrides are the governed exception. A failed or dataless gate can be overridden exactly once, with a mandatory reason, by the roles that hold the override authority. The row keeps reporting the underlying state; the override governs whether it still blocks, and the act is audited.
  • Metric-backed gates refuse manual submission — analytics and feedback own their values.

How the two interlock with feedback ​

The open-critical-bugs gate reads live feedback triage: unresolved critical reports block promotion into a ring that demands zero. Resolve the report and the next attempt re-evaluates and passes. That closed loop — report, triage, metric, gate, promotion — is covered in feedback and analytics.

Release policies (the standing rules) ​

Beside gates, an organization authors declarative IF/THEN rules that every promotion evaluates: metrics and gate states in the condition, BLOCK / HOLD_FOR_REVIEW / ALLOW in the action, scoped to the organization, an application, a ring, or the targeted environment. A fired HOLD_FOR_REVIEW rule parks the hop until a privileged acknowledge records the decision — with a reason — and that decision stands for later hops into the same ring. See rings and rollout.
