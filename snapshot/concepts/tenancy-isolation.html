Tenancy and isolation ​

ShipperDog is built for enterprises that distribute apps to many internal or customer populations at once. The tenancy model keeps each population's data separate while letting one organization govern everything centrally.

The hierarchy ​

text
Organization
├── Users (members with roles)
├── Tenants
│   ├── Members and testers
│   ├── Tester groups
│   └── Applications (tenant-owned)
├── Applications (organization-shared, optional)
├── Policies, rings, approval workflows
└── Audit logs
  • Organization — the top-level enterprise account. Platform administrators create organizations; the creator becomes its first organization administrator. Members hold organization roles (ORG_ADMIN, APP_OWNER, DEVELOPER, QA_ENGINEER, RELEASE_MANAGER, SECURITY_REVIEWER, COMPLIANCE_REVIEWER, VIEWER, and plain ORG_MEMBER).
  • Tenant — a customer or business entity inside the organization: "Acme Bank", "Contoso". Every tenant belongs to exactly one organization. A tenant is never a user — it has no login. Tenant identifiers are unique within the organization (name) and globally (external id, your billing/CRM mapping).
  • Application — belongs to a tenant, or is shared at the organization level. See below.
  • Testers — real people who install builds. A tester record belongs to the organization and is linked to tenants through memberships; testers do not need to be organization members.

Tenant-owned and organization-shared applications ​

An application has exactly one of two ownership scopes:

  • Tenant-owned — one owning tenant manages it. Other tenants see it only when explicitly authorized.
  • Organization-shared — the organization itself owns it (think: one corporate app every customer tenant receives). Which tenants can see it is managed through an explicit authorization mapping: grant with a reason, revoke the same way, everything audited.

Shared visibility is always opt-in and never ambient. Before a grant, a tenant's application list is exactly its own rows. The scope can be converted later, but not while releases target the application — distribution history anchors it.

Who can do what ​

Effective permissions are the union of every role a person holds across organization and tenant scopes:

  • Organization roles apply organization-wide. An ORG_ADMIN reaches into every tenant of their organization.
  • Tenant roles (TENANT_ADMIN, TENANT_TESTER) apply inside one tenant. Tenant testers — the tester persona — hold no other role at all: their access is membership plus explicit version grants.
  • Membership alone grants nothing (ORG_MEMBER is deny-by-default). A fresh member can do nothing until granted a functional role.

Memberships carry their own status besides the account status: both switches must be on. Suspending a membership takes effect on the very next request — roles are evaluated live from the database on every call, never baked into tokens.

Isolation: two layers, by construction ​

Tenant isolation is not a convention the API tries to remember. It is enforced twice:

  1. At the endpoint. Every tenant-scoped route checks the caller's standing. A caller without a role in the tenant (or its owning organization) receives the same 404 as if the tenant did not exist — a denied caller cannot distinguish "not yours" from "not there".
  2. At the data layer. Every tenant-scoped database query composes a tenant filter by construction. A query that would span tenants fails closed instead of running. Architecture tests reject any code path that bypasses the isolation layer.

Platform administrators can operate across tenants for break-glass operations, and every such request records an explicit, audited cross-tenant access event. Organization roles can never take that path.

Deactivation is reversible and non-destructive ​

  • A deactivated tenant blocks new memberships and affiliations but preserves everything: members, applications, and data remain readable and administrable so the tenant can be inspected and reactivated.
  • A deactivated organization keeps its memberships but they grant nothing; members whose organizations are all deactivated cannot sign in (platform administrators are exempt — that is the break-glass path).

Ring visibility ​

Tenants also interact with rollout rings: at a given ring, the tenants a release targets are intersected with the ring's eligibility rules, so widening a release stage by stage can admit tenant populations progressively. See rings and rollout.
